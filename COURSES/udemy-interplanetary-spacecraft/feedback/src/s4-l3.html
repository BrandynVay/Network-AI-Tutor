SEC 4 · LEC 3 · TEACH

Static electricity, pointed backward

Simple story first, then the official names, then how it actually works with numbers you can check.
🧒 Like you're 5
Rub a balloon on your hair and it pulls your hair toward it without touching. That pull is electricity grabbing things that are charged. An ion engine is that trick, aimed out the back. Step one: take a gas and knock one tiny piece (an electron) off each atom. The atom is now charged, and it is called an ion. Step two: put two metal screens full of holes at the back, one charged plus and one charged minus. They yank the ions through the holes and fling them out at 40 km a second, forty times faster than a rifle bullet. Step three: squirt the knocked-off electrons out as well, or the ship would end up charged and pull its own exhaust straight back.
The push is about the weight of two sheets of paper. But it never stops. A chemical rocket is the hare: one huge sprint, then it is out of breath. The ion engine is the tortoise: a tiny push, every second, for years, and it ends up going faster on a fifth of the propellant.

🏷 The official names (name first, then what it means)

  • Electric propulsion, EP (solar electric propulsion, SEP, when solar panels supply the power): electricity, not fire, accelerates the propellant.
  • Gridded ion thruster (electrostatic ion thruster, ion engine): the type in this lecture. Its cousin the Hall-effect thruster is the most common electric engine today and was not covered.
  • Ionization (here: electron-impact ionization): knocking an electron off a neutral atom. It costs the ionization energy, 12.1 eV for xenon.
  • Ion: an atom with a net charge. Lose an electron and it is a positive ion.
  • Plasma: gas hot or energetic enough that it is a mix of ions and free electrons. Overall it is still neutral. Often called the fourth state of matter.
  • Discharge chamber (ionization chamber): the can where the gas is ionized.
  • Discharge cathode (hollow cathode; the slide's "electron gun") and anode: the negative source of electrons and the positive wall that attracts them.
  • Potential difference (voltage): the electrical "height" between two points. Charges fall through it and speed up.
  • Ion optics: the pair of grids. Screen grid (the slide's "positive grid") and accelerator grid ("negative grid").
  • Neutralizer (neutralizer cathode; the slide's "neutralizing electron gun"): emits electrons into the ion beam.
  • Electron backstreaming: outside electrons rushing back into the engine. The negative grid blocks it.
  • Recombination: an ion and an electron rejoining into a neutral atom.
  • Primary propulsion / station-keeping / attitude control: the main Δv job / holding an orbit / pointing.

🛠 How it actually works

StepThe mechanism, with numbers (Deep Space 1's engine)
1 · FeedXenon gas trickles into the chamber: about 3 milligrams per second. Xenon because it is heavy, easy to ionize and does not react with anything.
2 · IonizeThe cathode emits electrons; about 30 volts speeds them up. One that hits a xenon atom hard enough (12.1 eV) knocks an electron off: now it is a positive ion. Magnets make the electrons spiral, so they travel far farther before reaching the wall and get many more chances to hit an atom.
3 · AccelerateScreen grid at +1,100 V, accelerator grid at −180 V, half a millimetre apart. An ion that drifts into a hole falls through that voltage: energy = charge × voltage = ½mv². For xenon, v = √(2qV ÷ m) ≈ 40 km/s.
4 · Where the push landsThe grids pull the ions backward-out; the ions pull the grids forward with the same force. Newton's third law again. The thrust is felt on the grids.
5 · NeutralizeThe beam carries off 1.76 amps of positive charge. Unbalanced, the ship would charge negative in microseconds and drag its own ions back: no thrust. The neutralizer emits the same 1.76 A of electrons.
6 · ThrustF = ṁ × vₑ ≈ 3 mg/s × 30 km/s = 0.092 N, 9 grams of push. (Effective exhaust is 30, not 40 km/s, because some gas leaves un-ionized.) Isp ≈ 3,100 s.
7 · Power is the limitThat 0.092 N took 2,300 W, a whole solar array. Thrust ≈ 2 × efficiency × power ÷ vₑ. For fixed power, faster exhaust means less thrust. About 40 mN per kW.
8 · Add it up0.092 N on 486 kg adds 16 m/s every day. Deep Space 1 thrust for 678 days in total: Δv 4.3 km/s from 74 kg of xenon. A chemical engine would need about 360 kg.

▲ Where the instructor was loose

  • "Electrons collide with atoms breaking their electronic bonds." No bonds are involved; xenon is single atoms. The collision knocks an electron off the atom. That is ionization.
  • "Plasma is… all of these particles which are positive." Plasma is the ions and the free electrons together, neutral overall. Only the ions get pulled out by the grids.
  • "The neutralizer… so the gas will freely disperse." The real reason is charge. Fire out only positive ions and the ship goes negative and pulls them back. This is the spacecraft charging from Section 2, self-inflicted.
  • "It cannot be used for launch… because of very low mass ejection." Closer: its thrust is thousands of times smaller than the craft's weight, and it needs vacuum to run.
  • "By 2020 half of all satellites will have electric propulsion." An old forecast that came true and more. Every Starlink has an electric thruster, and they are most of what gets launched.
  • BepiColombo "to perform orbital maneuvers around Mercury." Its ion engines are for the trip to Mercury, braking against the Sun's pull for years. That module is dropped before arrival; chemical thrusters do the capture. Arrival was planned for November 2026, which is right now. I can't confirm whether it has happened.
  • "Electron gun" and "positive / negative grid" are the diagram's labels. The engineering names are discharge cathode, screen grid and accelerator grid.
  • Not said: why low thrust. He says the mass flow is low. The cause is power: every milligram needs about 500 joules to reach 30 km/s, and the panels only supply so many joules per second.
  • Not said: the Hall thruster, the most common electric engine flying.

Orbital maintenance = station-keeping — small burns that hold an orbit against drift. Repeat, scene 1.

Ion / positive ion — an atom with a net charge / one that lost an electron.

Neutralizer = neutralizing electron gun — a small cathode that sprays electrons into the ion beam so the ship stays uncharged.
Neutralize / charge balance — make the + and − equal again.

🗣 The friend's opening question

"An engine that pushes as hard as two sheets of paper? How is that any use?"

✅ Three sentences you should be able to say

  • Because it never stops. It adds a few metres per second every day for years, and ends up faster than a chemical rocket on a fifth of the propellant.
  • It works like static electricity: knock an electron off each gas atom so it is charged, then two charged grids fling those ions out the back at 40 kilometres a second.
  • The catch is power. Throwing gas that fast takes a lot of electricity and solar panels only give so much, so the push is tiny and you can't launch or land with it.

❓ Follow-ups a friend might ask

  • "What's an ion?" (an atom that lost an electron, so electricity can grab it)
  • "What's plasma?" (gas where the atoms have been split into ions and loose electrons)
  • "Why spit the electrons out too?" (otherwise the ship charges up and pulls its exhaust back)
  • "Has it actually flown?" (since 1964; Deep Space 1 in 1998, Dawn, BepiColombo, every Starlink)
  • "Why not just add more solar panels?" (you can, but panels are mass, and far from the Sun they fade)
